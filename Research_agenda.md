# Projects

## 1. CROPDIVA ([https://cropdiva.eu](https://cropdiva.eu)) (2021–2026)

CROPDIVA concentrated on six underutilised or “orphan” crops (Oat, Hull-less barley, Narrow-leaved lupin, buckwheat, Tritricale, Faba bean) because diversifying what we grow is essential for a resilient and healthy food system. This has been the project I worked on at Agroscope throughout my PhD.

The tasks done consisted in:

- Supervising of a 3-year mixed cropping experiment (lentil & hull-less barley) across two Swiss sites, managing full experimental cycles from plot establishment to harvest and post-harvest analysis (laboratory analysis of grain quality parameters - beta-glucan content, mycotoxin content - and grain separation performance).
- Conducted agronomic and morphological field observations of a spring and winter barley diversity panel of 300+ genotypes, complemented by image-based canopy cover analysis (Canopeo) and post-harvest NIR spectroscopy.
- Quantified provisioning and regulating ecosystem services (pollination, weed suppression, yield stability) delivered by four underutilized crops under mono- and mixed cropping systems.
- Producing manuscripts and presenting results at national and international conferences.

Keywords: **Niche crops, intercropping, protein crops, diversifications**

### 2. “IC2field”

Intercropping experiments often lack a systemic vision that addresses not only agronomic and ecological aspects, but also **gene expression, soil microbiology, pedoclimatic conditions, computational analysis for crop model calibration, and, most importantly, farmers**. How can all these aspects be connected harmoniously?

I am exploring how intercropping experiments could be approached from a more **systemic perspective**, connecting processes and disciplines that are often investigated separately.

**Can we develop better ways of understanding the complexity of diversified cropping systems?**

Keywords: **Transcriptomic, agroecology, soil microbiology, crop modelling**

### 3. “Social pace and biological pace”

Technology is accelerating almost everything around us: communication, information, transportation, entertainment, consumption, and even the sensory environments we inhabit. This acceleration has become so ordinary that we rarely notice it.

But our biology has not been designed at technological speed.

**What happens when the environment changes faster than the organism?**

Keywords: **Social acceleration · Biological rhythms · Human adaptation**
