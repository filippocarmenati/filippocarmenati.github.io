# Hi there! Were you looking for me?

**Benvenuto!**

I am Filippo Carmenati, an agroecologist specializing in intercropping and interested in designing diversified cropping systems through a systems agroecology approach. My involvement in this field has taken me on an exciting journey, both intellectually and geographically.

I first became interested in alternative cropping systems during a course I took while on Erasmus at CULS in 2017. Driven by curiosity, I went on to study sustainable agriculture, with a particular focus on intercropping systems. My research has ranged from the historical use of intercropping and crop rotations, which was the topic of my B.Sc. thesis, to variation in functional traits of cocoa under monoculture and agroforestry systems during my M.Sc. thesis. I later moved toward farm-scale experiments investigating underutilized crops and their integration into diversified cropping systems during my Ph.D. at Agroscope and ETH Zürich.

**Diversity and interactions among system components have been the common thread connecting these experiences and continue to fuel my curiosity.**

I have been fortunate to work with leading European research institutes and, above all, with wonderful people throughout my academic journey.

While I am passionate about agroecology, I am equally interested in working across disciplines and with people from different backgrounds. My interests extend beyond agroecology to **soil microbiology, computational science,** and **plant biology**. On the other side of the coin, I am also curious **about art, social sciences, and anthropology**. I see these fields as offering complementary perspectives on interaction, diversity, and the relationships among components that can make a system richer and more resilient when thoughtfully designed.

Feel free to “flip the coin” and explore some of my interdisciplinary ideas. Perhaps some of them could be developed together.
